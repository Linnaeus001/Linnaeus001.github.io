About & Research Interests

I am an evolutionary entomologist broadly interested in understanding how microevolutionary processes translate into functional phenotypic adaptations and macroevolutionary divergence. My research integrates systematic entomology, geometric morphometrics, and ecological genomics to resolve species limits, dissect structural morphological variation, and reconstruct phylogenetic histories.

Drawing inspiration from functional paradigms such as monarch butterfly migration—where long-distance flight mechanics, wing morphology, and underlying genomic architecture intersect—I investigate how functional phenotypes co-evolve with genomic variation across spatio-temporal scales. By combining high-resolution geometric morphometrics with next-generation sequencing (including non-destructive museomics for historical specimens), I test reproductive isolation hypotheses (e.g., the "Lock & Key" mechanism vs. sexual selection) and explore both extant radiations and extinct fossil lineages preserved in amber.
